<h2 class="highlight">
Introduction
</h2>

I am a postdoctoral researcher in high-energy particle physics with over a decade of research experience spanning cosmology, mathematical quantum field theory, experimental high energy physics, and detector operation and design. I also have a passion for teaching and knowledge transfer, having taught undergraduate physics courses and led advanced data-analysis workshops within the CMS collaboration. After greatly enjoying recent work maintaining a sub-detector for the CMS experiment and prototyping an electron detection system for the planned LUXE experiment, I am eager to continue my career while moving to an applied physics focus.

<h2 class="highlight">
Postdoctoral research experience
</h2>

**Postdoctoral Fellow at Deutsches Elektronen-Synchrotron (DESY), Hamburg** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2022–

**Work with the CMS experiment (Large Hadron Collider, CERN):**
- Luminosity measurement via silicon-diode-based detector, including operational work for the BCM1F luminometer, data acquisition code maintenance, and end-of-year data reprocessing for precision luminosity estimates
- Data analysis with a focus on top quark physics, including a lead role in the first physics publication of LHC Run 3 (top quark pair production cross section at 13.6 TeV)

**Work with the LUXE planned strong-field QED experiment (DESY):**
- Simulation of a novel segmented Cherenkov detector for high-rate electron detection and spectrometry, including detailed detector simulation in Geant4 and radiation level studies in FLUKA
- Validation of detector prototypes with electron beams at the ARES linear accelerator (DESY) and at the FACET-II facility (SLAC, in coordination with the E320 experiment)

**Postdoctoral Research Associate with Prof. Arie Bodek, University of Rochester** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2020–2022

- Developing new methods using machine learning to identify photons and electrons traversing the forward region of the CMS electromagnetic and hadronic calorimeters
- Precision measurements of the electroweak mixing angle at the CMS experiment
