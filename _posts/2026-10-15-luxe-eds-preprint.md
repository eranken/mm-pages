Another paper I contributed to is now publicly available as a pre-print! This paper details design prototyping and testing for the electron detection system of the proposed [LUXE experiment](https://luxe.desy.de/).

This detector went through multiple rounds of prototyping and testing. In particular, in late 2024 I had the great pleasure of traveling to SLAC with some of my DESY coworkers to test the latest prototype at the [FACET-II facility](https://facet-ii.slac.stanford.edu/). Working closely with the accelerator team, we were able to take essential calibration data and even some "parasitic" data during the operation of the [E320 experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04992). It was an unforgettable experience and a lot of work!

These tests were invaluable to inform the final design of the electron detection system, as the FACET-II facility is in many ways similar to the planned environment for the LUXE experiment. In non-technical terms, the electrons produced at FACET-II have a similar energy, and come in large numbers bunched together--similar to what we expect at LUXE. Furthermore, the E320 experiment has similar physics goals to the planned LUXE experiment, colliding the electron beam with high-intensity laser pulses. Taking data simultaneously with the E-320 experiment gave us a chance to use the detector in the most "realistic" test-run possible.

The technical details can all be found at the link above! Thanks to everyone who worked on this and to the [EAJADE program](https://www.eajade.eu/) for supporting our trip!
